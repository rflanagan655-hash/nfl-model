Game lines: leans and EV

The model's projected margin blends an independent team rating with the consensus market spread. The rating mixes ESPN's Football Power Index with an efficiency line built from 2026 EPA per play: each team's expected EPA per snap is its offense plus the opponent's defense, worth about 31 points per 1.0 EPA after halving early-season noise, plus 1.5 points of home field. Each side's cover and win probability is then priced against every DraftKings, FanDuel and Fanatics number, and the best-priced side with positive expected value is the lean. Shaded rows are games involving your starters. Totals use the same efficiency matchup: the model total moves part of the way from the market total toward the efficiency total (two offenses against two defenses, less wind and rain), and over/under are priced at the standard -110 because the totals prices aren't loaded.

At 0% rating weight, 0% totals weight and all signals off, the model equals the market and no lean survives the vig. Spreads: the rating weight was cut from 50% to 30% on Nov 5 (the model's margins had missed by slightly more than the closing spread), and a spread or moneyline only counts as a lean when the model's margin differs from the market spread by at least the minimum gap (both come from the same margin); smaller differences were close to coin flips. Totals are unchanged.

Lines, splits and injury statuses refresh automatically each morning through Monday's game; the "Open → now" column logs each day's spread.

Betting-market signals

Each signal nudges the projected margin by a few tenths of a point. Set any weight to 0 to switch it off. Money vs tickets: when a side's share of the money is well above its share of bets, larger (often sharper) bettors are on it; up to ±2 pts at a 100-point gap. Line move: 0.15 pts per point the spread has moved since open, capped at 1. Reverse line move: 0.5 pts toward a side when 60%+ of tickets are on the other side and the line still moved its way. Fade the public: 0.3 pts against a side with 70%+ of tickets. ATS trend: 1 pt × the gap in each team's 2026 cover rate; trends like this have little predictive value, so it starts low. ATS STATS role: the 2026 ATS STATS NFL Betting Guide names a "best role" and an "avoid role" for every team (home or road, favorite or underdog; "short" means 3.5 points or less, "inflated", "large" or "big" 7 or more). A team in its best role gets +0.5 pts, in its avoid role −0.5; labels with conditions the model can't check ("vs elite teams", "after long winning streaks") and totals-only labels are skipped. ATS STATS sentiment: up to ±0.5 pts from the gap in the guide's confidence scores (Bullish 100%, Neutral 33–66%, Bearish 0%). The guide is a preseason rating built from 2023–25 results with no published track record, so both stay small and can be turned off; the tracker will show whether they help. Gridiron Games' ATS trends are behind a subscription and aren't used.

Margin is from the home team's side (positive = home wins by). Spread cover uses a normal margin distribution with a 13.5-point standard deviation and counts pushes on whole-number lines; win probability starts from the moneylines with the bookmaker's margin removed by the power method (books load more margin onto the underdog, so splitting it evenly overstates underdogs), averaged over the books whose spread matches the consensus, then shifts by the model's edge over the market spread at 10.5 points per standard deviation. A book's moneyline more than 8 points of win probability away from that market number, or its spread more than 3 points off the consensus, is treated as stale and skipped. A moneyline longer than +400 needs +$10 of EV to count as a lean. Top 10 props this week

The model's ten best player props for the week, ranked by expected value per $100. Every posted DraftKings and Fanatics over and under for every player the model prices is checked against 15,000 simulated games; a bet makes the list only if the model's probability is at least 3 points above the book's (after removing the vig) and its EV is positive, with one bet per player and market (its best line, side and book) and only games that haven't kicked off. Projections use each player's season carry, target, red-zone and snap shares and the opponent's yards and EPA allowed. Model probabilities assume the player is active, since books void props for players who don't play. The list resets with each new week and updates as books post more props. Bets where the model is above 85% or more than 25 points above the book are left off: gaps that big almost always mean an input is stale (a role change, an injury limit) rather than a real edge. Players coming back from injury are where the model is most likely to be overconfident.

College football

Spreads, totals and moneylines from DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM and Fanatics for the week's featured college games. The model starts from DraftKings' line with the bookmaker's margin removed by the power method (which, unlike splitting the margin evenly, doesn't overstate underdogs' chances), adjusts for home field, unpriced injuries, ATS trends and weather, then moves 15% of the way toward a ratings-only projection built from SP+ offense and defense ratings, yards per play gained and allowed, pace (plays per game, which with yards per play makes up yards per game), turnover rates (takeaways and giveaways) and explosive plays (20+ yard plays produced and allowed), with margin and total each modeled as a normal distribution, then prices every side at every book on that book's own number. Each bet shows the book with the best EV. EV is the average profit per $100 if the model's probability is right. Grades: Strong lean +$5 or more, Lean +$3, Slight +$1. Moneylines longer than +400 need +$10 of EV to count as a bet or a game's best bet, since small errors in a longshot's chances swing its EV the most. Prices move, so confirm the number at the book before betting.

How it's computed. (1) Team touchdowns: each team's implied points from the betting market, moved 25% toward the EPA efficiency matchup (its offense vs the opponent's defense), × 0.105 touchdowns per point. (2) Rush vs pass split: half the team's own 2026 rush/pass TD mix (play-calling tendency), half what the opponent has allowed, tilted toward whichever of the opponent's run or pass defense is weaker by EPA per play, plus game script (favorites run more). Both are regressed toward the league mix. (3) Player share: for rush TDs, 35% season carry share, 40% red-zone carry share, 25% share of team rush TDs this season (smoothed); for receiving TDs, the same with targets and red-zone targets. QB rushing counts at a discount because scrambles rarely score. (4) Snap rate nudges players on the field more (±10%). (5) Injuries: P(active) from the latest injury report; a ruled-out or doubtful teammate's share moves to the others at his position. P(TD) = P(active) × (1 − e−expected TDs). Fair odds are the price that would break even at that probability; where a book's anytime TD price is loaded in Props, the best one is shown with its EV.

Model Tracker: game lines

Every game's model line and lean is logged before kickoff (the last update before the game is the one that counts), then graded against the final score. Leans are the bets the model flags: NFL games with a lean above +$2 EV per $100 in the Game lines section, and every college bet graded Slight or better. They are graded at the price and book logged, risking 1 unit each. Best bet every game grades each game's single highest-EV bet across spread, moneyline and total (the college section's "Best bet" column), at its logged price and book, whether or not it cleared the lean threshold. Every-game picks grade the model's side on every game whether or not it cleared the threshold. At standard -110 prices a bettor needs 52.4% to break even.

How the model works

Market environmentImplied team points = total ÷ 2 − spread ÷ 2, using the market total and spread moved 25% toward the efficiency matchup (each offense's EPA per play against the opposing defense's, 2026 season to date). Plays scale with implied points, and touchdowns ≈ 0.105 × implied points, split between passing and rushing by team tendency.
Game scriptPass rate moves 0.6 points per point of spread: favorites run more, underdogs throw more.
WeatherWind above 12 mph cuts pass rate 0.4 points and passing yards 1% per mph; rain at 40% or more trims another 3%. Domes are unaffected.
Usage and matchupUsage is data-driven: each player's recent-role rush share, target share and TD shares are blended with his 2026 season-to-date carry share, target share, red-zone carry/target share and snap rate (data weight = games ÷ (games + 5), so about half after five games). Snap rate also sets how steady his volume is week to week. Matchups are data-driven: the opponent's 2026 passing and rushing yards allowed per game, EPA per pass and per rush allowed, and pass/rush TDs allowed (vs league average, regressed for sample size) scale yards per attempt, catch and carry, pass attempts and carries, and the pass/rush TD split.
Simulation15,000 games with shared team-volume and game-scoring noise, Poisson touchdowns and targets, binomial catches and lognormal yardage.
Data qualityPlayers tagged researched use 2026 usage and injury reporting. Players tagged role estimate or added start from a role preset; adjust their shares if you know their recent usage.
