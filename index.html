Game lines: leans and EV

The model's projected margin blends an independent team rating with the consensus market spread. The rating mixes ESPN's Football Power Index with an efficiency line built from 2026 EPA per play: each team's expected EPA per snap is its offense plus the opponent's defense, worth about 31 points per 1.0 EPA after halving early-season noise, plus 1.5 points of home field. Each side's cover and win probability is then priced against every DraftKings, FanDuel and Fanatics number, and the best-priced side with positive expected value is the lean. Shaded rows are games involving your starters. Totals use the same efficiency matchup: the model total moves part of the way from the market total toward the efficiency total (two offenses against two defenses, less wind and rain), and over/under are priced at the standard -110 because the totals prices aren't loaded.

At 0% rating weight, 0% totals weight and all signals off, the model equals the market and no lean survives the vig.

Lines, splits and injury statuses refresh automatically each morning through Monday's game; the "Open → now" column logs each day's spread.

Betting-market signals

Each signal nudges the projected margin by a few tenths of a point. Set any weight to 0 to switch it off. Money vs tickets: when a side's share of the money is well above its share of bets, larger (often sharper) bettors are on it; up to ±2 pts at a 100-point gap. Line move: 0.15 pts per point the spread has moved since open, capped at 1. Reverse line move: 0.5 pts toward a side when 60%+ of tickets are on the other side and the line still moved its way. Fade the public: 0.3 pts against a side with 70%+ of tickets. ATS trend: 1 pt × the gap in each team's 2026 cover rate; trends like this have little predictive value, so it starts low.

College football

Spreads, totals and moneylines from DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM and Fanatics for the week's featured college games. The model starts from DraftKings' margin-free line, adjusts for home field, unpriced injuries, ATS trends and weather, then moves 15% of the way toward a ratings-only projection built from SP+ offense and defense ratings, turnover rates (takeaways and giveaways) and explosive plays (20+ yard plays produced and allowed), with margin and total each modeled as a normal distribution, then prices every side at every book on that book's own number. Each bet shows the book with the best EV. EV is the average profit per $100 if the model's probability is right. Grades: Strong lean +$5 or more, Lean +$3, Slight +$1. Prices move, so confirm the number at the book before betting.

    How the model works

    Market environmentImplied team points = total ÷ 2 − spread ÷ 2, using the market total and spread moved 25% toward the efficiency matchup (each offense's EPA per play against the opposing defense's, 2026 season to date). Plays scale with implied points, and touchdowns ≈ 0.105 × implied points, split between passing and rushing by team tendency.
    Game scriptPass rate moves 0.6 points per point of spread: favorites run more, underdogs throw more.
    WeatherWind above 12 mph cuts pass rate 0.4 points and passing yards 1% per mph; rain at 40% or more trims another 3%. Domes are unaffected.
    Usage and matchupEach player has a rush share, target share, efficiency and TD share, scaled by a snap factor and a defensive matchup multiplier (1.00 = average defense).
    Simulation15,000 games with shared team-volume and game-scoring noise, Poisson touchdowns and targets, binomial catches and lognormal yardage.
    Data qualityPlayers tagged researched use 2026 usage and injury reporting. Players tagged role estimate or added start from a role preset; adjust their shares if you know their recent usage.
